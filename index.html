The Core Idea

Wellness therapies have a delivery issue. BAM solves it with hardware.

The $6.8 trillion wellness space is built on apps, journals, and willpower. It asks people to think their way toward feeling better. That approach doesn't reach everyone.

BAM takes a different path. We build physical, living environments that settle the nervous system through the senses — without needing willpower or motivation. You don't have to try to feel better. The space does the work.

WHY IT WORKS

The brain has two reward systems. One spikes and crashes — that's the loop we're all trying to leave behind. The other plateaus and holds — that's serotonin. BAM is designed to build the second. Small daily tending becomes a keystone habit that ripples into everything else. Over weeks, the nervous system learns a new baseline: low, steady, and self-sustaining.

Common Questions

FAQ

What makes BAM hard to copy?

Three things. First, our method and terminology are protected (trademarks pending). Second, our community — the Anchor Club — keeps members involved through accountability partners and streak tracking. Third, the founder's lived recovery experience and forensic intelligence background can't be copied. On top of that, we source materials at zero cost from local stone yards, so competitors can't match our pricing.

How does BAM fit into the wellness space?

We're a peer-supported wellness practice. Every video, product, and consultation includes clear guidance. We support nervous system regulation — we don't replace medical care. Every participant is screened carefully, and our Ethics Charter is public and enforced. This keeps our work responsible, transparent, and safe.

What are the numbers per product?

Physical products carry 80–97% gross margins. Anchor Stones cost about $0.30 and sell for $12. Desktop Sanctuary Jars cost $6–8 and sell for $35–45. Consultations are $75 with almost no additional cost. Anchor Club is $8 per month with 90%+ margin. Each expo nets about $5,850 with no upfront capital.

Why a for-profit plus charity structure?

Three reasons. First, it opens grants that a for-profit can't apply for. Second, it allows tax-deductible donations from donors, including gifts of appreciated securities with no capital gains tax. Third, it gives credibility with clinical partners. The for-profit arm pays salaries and scales commercially. The charity arm runs mission programs and receives non-dilutive funding.

What is the exit path?

Three options. (1) Acquisition by a large wellness or pet company entering the bio-architecture space (PetSmart, Chewy, Mars Petcare). (2) Acquisition by a corporate wellness provider seeking physical hardware (LifeSpeak, CoreHealth). (3) Independent operation as a profitable, founder-owned business with a charity arm. We're building for option 3 — with options 1 and 2 as upside.
