The Framework

From the Window of Tolerance to Predictive Coherence™

BAM does not rely on outdated Polyvagal models. It is grounded in Predictive Processing and Interoception. Here is the clinical architecture.

1. The Window of Tolerance

Trauma and chronic stress shrink your nervous system's capacity for arousal. When pushed outside this "window," you either shut down (dissociate) or panic (hypervigilance). BAM is designed to gently widen this window.

2. Ecological Interoceptive Therapy™ (E.I.T.)

Our clinical modality. By tending to a living ecosystem, you train your brain to accurately read internal signals (interoception). The rhythm of the living system co-regulates your autonomic nervous system.

The Goal: Predictive Coherence™

When your brain's predictions (what it expects to feel) match the sensory evidence (safety, rhythm, nature), the threat system powers down. You enter a state of Predictive Coherence™—absolute, grounded presence.

Daily Practice 01

Observe

Watch your ecosystem. No phone. No agenda. Just soft attention on movement, colour, and breath. This anchors you in the present moment.

Daily Practice 02

Tend

Mist, feed, remove a leaf. A small, predictable protocol of care. Low dopamine. No crash. This builds interoceptive agency.

Daily Practice 03

Integrate

The outside world starts to look like the inside of your terrarium. Your nervous system learns to find safety in the natural world, not just the jar.

Age 14 · Student

Maya

School days. Homework. Group chats always on.
Where she started

A racing mind before tests. Trouble winding down at night. The feeling that everyone else had it figured out.

What changed

A sealed moss jar on her desk. One minute of watching the springtails before bed. Now she notices moss on the walk to school and feels the same quiet.

Age 38 · Marketing Director

Daniel

Remote work. 55-hour weeks. Coffee dependency. Notifications around the clock.
Where he started

A mind that wouldn't stop. Poor sleep. Snapping at people he loved. Always on, never settled.

What changed

A bioactive terrarium on his desk. Three anchor glances a day. Now he pauses at puddles after rain. Same calm. Different location.

Lives alone. Grandchildren visit on weekends. Some mornings feel shapeless.

Days that blurred together. Nothing needed her. She described it as waiting.

What changed

A low-maintenance ecosystem and a polished mineral set. Now she tends her garden the same way she tends her jar. One leaf at a time. Nothing rushed.
